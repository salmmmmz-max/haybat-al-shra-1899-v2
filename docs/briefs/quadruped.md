## The R&D at the heart of it
`camel.glb` and `wolf.glb` are STATIC textured meshes (no skeleton): camel — cream-white riding camel with a sadu saddle blanket and a wooden saddle frame, 2.55 m tall, 2.54 m long, faces +Z, 11.4k triangles (LOD1 2.3k); wolf — 1.2 m long, 8.5k triangles (LOD1 1.7k); both normalised by `G.assets.instance(id)` (feet on y = 0, centred, facing +Z, metres; manifest `size`). You give them life: build a skeleton at load time, compute skin weights from vertex positions, and animate procedurally. A camel that glides without moving its legs would ruin the game — this is the single most important piece of character work in the project.
- Inspect the meshes first. `node tools/render-glb.cjs ../app/public/assets/models/camel.glb .cache/out/camel.png 1200 800` prints stats and writes a multi-view contact sheet. For measurements (leg x/z positions, belly height, hump, neck, head, tail), analyse the vertex cloud inside a harness scene and `--print` JSON (histograms of slices) rather than guessing.
- Rig (once per model, cached): bake the template to a Float32 geometry in metres; ≤ 24 bones — root, pelvis, spine/chest, neck ×2–3, head, tail, and per leg: upper, lower, foot (camel legs are long: hip/shoulder, knee/hock, fetlock); weights = smooth blend of the 2 nearest bone SEGMENTS with legs separated by side and by front/rear (a left-front vertex must never follow a right or a hind bone); the saddle, blanket and load follow the spine rigidly (no rubbery saddle). Explicit bounding sphere. LOD1 uses the same vertex buffers (skin attributes included) with the 20 % index (`G.assets.lodGeometry(id)`) through `G.lod.add`.
- Motion = procedural gait cycles (summed sinusoids / small IK), stride length tied to ground speed (no foot sliding), body bob, weight shift, neck counter-swing, tail sway, breathing and ear/head idles. **Camel = pace** (both legs of one side move together; the characteristic rolling sway), walk 2.4 m/s, trot 5.5 m/s. **Wolf**: trot ≈ 3 m/s (diagonal pairs), bounding run ≈ 8 m/s.
- Poses (`body.pose(name, { duration })` → Promise through `G.scope`): camel `kneel` (front knees fold, then the hind legs, the body settles on the ground: saddle ≈ 1.0 m lower) · `rise` (reverse: hind first) · `shake` · `raiseHead` · `lowerHead`; wolf `crouch` · `lunge` (a 3–4 m leap) · `snarl` · `howl` · `cower`. `body.kneeling` (bool). Poses blend with the gait system; `fastText` → ≤ 0.1 s.
- `body.saddle` per glue 9; `body.head` follows the head bone; `body.lookAt(target | null)` turns neck and head (≤ 70°), damped.
- `rig: 'static'` (and the automatic fallback if a rig fails): rigid body with bob/pitch only — for distant caravan members. A kneeling camel that will not move for a while may be swapped for the static `camelsit` prop mesh? No — `camelsit` is a different (loaded pack) camel; instead keep the kneeling pose and stop updating its skeleton (`body.freeze(true)`) so it costs nothing.
- Budget: ≤ 12 skinned meshes on screen in the whole game (people included) — camels beyond ~25 m switch to `static`; frozen/static bodies skip skeleton updates. No per-frame allocation.
- Tints: الوضحا keeps the original cream-white; pack camels get darker fawn/brown tints (`setTint`); the calf «الحوار» is the camel at scale 0.55; the alpha wolf «الأمعط» is the wolf at ×1.25, darker.

## camels.js
`camels.spawn(id, { x, z, yaw, scale, tint, rig, model })` → camel = body + a small brain: `goTo` (promise), `follow(target, { dist, speed })`, `flee(from, dist)` (promise), `stop()`, idle behaviours (shift weight, look around, chew). Suspended while `camel.ridden` (glue 9). Ground = `terrain.heightAt`; body pitch follows the slope a little. Avoids `G.world.props.colliders` crudely. `camels.all`, `get`, `remove`, `clear`, `onLevelUnload`.

## caravan.js
`form({ lead, camels: n, tints, walkers: [npcId], riders: [{ npc, index }] })` spawns `n` pack camels (ids `caravan0…`) in a string behind `lead` (a camel object — usually the ridden الوضحا). `follow(true)`: each member follows the breadcrumb of the one in front at 4.5 m spacing (the string snakes through turns exactly along the lead's path); walkers (`G.actors.npcs.get(id)`) walk beside the string with `npc.followTrail`-like behaviour that YOU drive via `npc.goTo`/`npc.follow`; riders sit via `npc.ride(camel)`. `caravan.pace` → the lead-speed cap: 2.4 on firm sand, 1.8 when the lead is on soft sand (`terrain.softAt`) — on soft sand the string visibly stretches and lags. `halt()`, `kneelAll({ ring?: { x, z, r } })` → Promise (members move to slots on the ring — or stop in line — and kneel one after another), `riseAll()` → Promise, `members` (array), `clear()`.

## wolves.js (chapter 3, night, by the fire)
- `spawnPack({ cx, cz, radius = 14, arc: { centerYaw, width } })`: the fire is at (cx, cz). At the rim of the firelight: 5–6 wolves shown only as pairs of glowing eyes + dark silhouettes (ONE instanced eye mesh + static LOD1 bodies or dark cards), slowly pacing along the arc. `wolves.threat = true`.
- `wave({ target, lunges, gap, double = false })` → `Promise<{ repelled, missed }>`: `lunges` attacks `gap` seconds apart (the last two 0.6 s apart from adjacent slots when `double`). For each: pick a rim slot with `G.rng` (never opposite sides in a row: the next slot is within 100° of the previous one); set `wolves.next` 2.5 s before the telegraph (its eyes glint with `G.sys.firasa.amount`); telegraph 1.2 s — emit `wolf:telegraph { wolf, x, z, in: 1.2 }`, eyes flare, the ONE full skinned wolf steps into the light there and crouches; then `wolf:lunge` — it leaps toward `target` (an Object3D or `{x, z}`: the calf). It is repelled iff a `player:swing { x, z, yaw }` happened within the last 1.1 s whose `yaw` points within ± 60° of the wolf as seen from the swing position → `wolf:repelled` (yelp, it tumbles back into the dark); otherwise `wolf:bite` and `missed++`. Only one full wolf is in the light at a time.
- `alphaEnter()` (الأمعط walks slowly out of the dark to 6 m from the player and stares; `wolves.alpha` = its body) · `alphaFeint()` (the same 1.2 s telegraph + a 1 m false lunge, then back) · `alphaLunge()` (the punishment for a flinch: `wolf:bite`) · `flee()` (the alpha turns away; the eye pairs go out one by one; resolves when all are gone; `threat = false`) · `clear()` / `onBeatEnd()` / `onLevelUnload()`.
- Everything through `G.rng` and `G.scope`; `fastText` shortens every wait to ≤ 0.1 s (lunges still emit their events in order).

## Harness

Deliver contact sheets that prove the legs really walk: camel walk + trot (side and front views, 6 phases each), the kneel sequence (5 frames), wolf trot/run/crouch/lunge, the caravan on a curve, the wolf pack at the rim of the firelight.
